| Tuning | Both BLRs and the tuned GP search the same 40 signal amplitudes using training evidence. The GP additionally searches 50 lengthscales. The untuned GP deliberately keeps `(lengthscale, sf) = (0.5, 0.01)`. |

| Uncertainty target | Height-comparison bands and coverage concern **new noisy observations**. The derivative plot and MPC use **latent slope uncertainty**. Height observation noise is never differentiated or added to slope variance. |
| Control comparisons | Part 2 compares tuned/untuned GP controllers with common task, constraints, horizons and costs. It does not compare BLR-MPC and GP-MPC. Chapter 6.2 is not an equivalent control baseline. |

Equal marginal amplitudes do not make different kernels identical. For the BLR basis, its function-space kernel is `phi(p).T @ Sigma0 @ phi(p')`. BLR and GP with that same kernel and mean must agree; an explicit numerical test confirms that they do. The observed gap difference is therefore caused by the chosen structural priors. This equivalence and the posterior/noisy-observation distinction follow the weight-space and function-space derivations in [Rasmussen and Williams, Chapter 2](https://gaussianprocess.org/gpml/chapters/RW2.pdf).

**Implementation findings and fixes**

1. **BLR nonzero prior mean could broadcast incorrectly.** A one-dimensional `mu0` combined with a column-vector likelihood term could produce a coefficient matrix. Means are now normalized to columns and prior dimensions checked. The posterior retains the original explicit-inverse equations for readability; prior validation and the column-vector shape fix are kept. `predict(..., include_noise=False)` now exposes latent uncertainty, while the default retains the existing noisy-observation behavior. Evidence is implemented alongside fitting rather than duplicated in the notebook.
2. **GP predictions could silently combine incompatible hyperparameters and cached factors.** Changing a lengthscale after fitting changed test kernels but left the old training factorization in use. Prediction and CasADi export now require the fitted hyperparameters to match; tuning invalidates the cached fit. Posterior solves use Cholesky factors. CasADi and NumPy variances both clamp numerical negative latent variances before adding optional observation noise.
3. **The slope plot centered its band on zero.** It now plots the posterior derivative mean and that mean ± three derivative standard deviations. The derivative covariance formula itself was correct: it uses the mixed derivative of the full posterior covariance, not a derivative of pointwise height variance.
4. **Acceleration variance dropped a covariance term.** Both propulsion and gravity depend on the same uncertain slope `z`. The correct first-order variance is `(u*c'(z) - g*(s(z)*c(z))')² Var[z]`. The old expression added separate squared sensitivities, incorrectly treating these terms as independent. The corrected expression is checked against Monte Carlo draws for both input signs.
5. **MPC stage-zero reference and solver failures.** Stage zero now receives the same reference as other running-cost stages. A nonzero acados status raises an error rather than returning an unverified control action. The stage-zero state-cost difference was constant because the initial state is fixed, so it was not the cause of the previous trajectories.
6. **Speed diagnostics checked only the positive bound.** The report now checks `abs(v)` against the symmetric speed constraint. These are sampled violations, not continuous-time guarantees.
7. **Claims exceeded the experiment.** The notebook no longer describes a grid winner as a continuous global optimum, the true-model controller as a performance ceiling, a tuned GP as universally calibrated, or the ratio of two slope standard deviations as a ratio of actual error or tightening margins. Old hardcoded analysis numbers were replaced with interpretation of the freshly computed tables.

Evidence integrates out coefficients/function values at specified hyperparameters. Maximizing it and then reporting it on the same data is empirical Bayes; it is not hyperparameter marginalization or held-out validation. The report and notebook now state this distinction and the dependence on priors/search bounds. See [Rasmussen and Williams, Chapter 5](https://gaussianprocess.org/gpml/chapters/RW5.pdf) for model selection and marginal likelihood.

Remaining limitations are stated in the notebook:

- The controller injects model error independently each step, ignoring temporal correlations of a static uncertain terrain and state-model cross-correlations. Discretized noise injection is an Euler approximation.
- Ancillary feedback is assumed only in covariance prediction. It is not the implemented tube policy or a verified MPC policy sensitivity. Soft constraints and capped margins further prevent a literal chance-constraint guarantee. Beta 2 gives 95.45% central coverage for a scalar Gaussian, not 95% joint trajectory coverage.
- This is one terrain and training seed. Coverage diagnostics do not establish calibration across terrains, training sets, or derivatives; empirical-Bayes fits omit hyperparameter uncertainty.
- The legacy Chapter 6.2 path still falls back to differentiating pointwise height variance if no slope covariance is supplied. That fallback is not a correct derivative variance and was outside this notebook's active path; Chapter 6.3 explicitly supplies the correct slope covariance. The older BLR-MPC experiment is not certified by this review.
